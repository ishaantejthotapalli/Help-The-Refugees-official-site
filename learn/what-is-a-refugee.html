What is a refugee?

A refugee is someone who has left their country because they face serious danger there and need protection in another country.

Published and reviewed 26 July 2026 · About 5 minutes to read

Why might someone leave home?

People usually do not choose to become refugees. They may leave because of war, violence, or persecution connected to who they are, what they believe, or the group they belong to. Leaving can mean saying goodbye to a home, school, friends and familiar places.

Important: Refugees are people first. They have families, interests, skills, hopes and plans. The word “refugee” describes a legal and protection situation; it does not describe a person's character.

What does protection mean?

International protection means a refugee should not be sent back to a place where their life or freedom would be threatened. Refugees also have human rights, including access to safety, education and essential services. The exact process is different in each country.

Do all displaced people cross a border?

No. Someone forced to leave home who remains inside their own country is called an internally displaced person. They may face many similar dangers and needs, but they have not crossed an international border.

How can we speak respectfully?

  • Say “a person who is a refugee” or “refugee children,” not labels that remove someone's individuality.
  • Do not assume every person has the same story.
  • Listen to people describing their own experiences.
  • Check facts before sharing dramatic images or claims.
