Refugee, migrant and asylum seeker: what is the difference?

These words are related, but they do not mean the same thing. Using the right word helps us understand what protection a person may need.

Published and reviewed 26 July 2026 · About 6 minutes to read

Refugee

A refugee has crossed an international border because returning home would expose them to serious danger or persecution. Refugees are protected under international and national law.

Asylum seeker

An asylum seeker is asking another country to recognise that they need protection. Their claim is still being considered. A person should not be described as dishonest or illegal simply because a decision has not yet been made.

Migrant

There is no single universal legal definition of “migrant.” The word commonly describes someone who moves, often for work, study, family or other reasons. Some migrants also move in difficult circumstances, but the specific international protection attached to refugee status is different.

Internally displaced person

An internally displaced person has been forced to leave home but remains within their own country's borders. They have not crossed an international border.

Why the words matter

Calling every person on the move a migrant can hide a refugee's need for international protection. At the same time, every person—whatever their legal category—has human rights and deserves dignity.
