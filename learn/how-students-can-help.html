How can students help refugees?

Students can help by learning carefully, welcoming new classmates, challenging stereotypes and supporting actions led by trusted organisations.

Published and reviewed 26 July 2026 · About 5 minutes to read

Be welcoming without demanding a story

A new classmate may appreciate a tour, an invitation to join an activity, or help understanding a school routine. Do not pressure anyone to explain why they left home. Friendship should not depend on sharing private or painful experiences.

Learn before you share

  • Use information from UNHCR, UNICEF and established humanitarian organisations.
  • Check the publication date and reporting year of statistics.
  • Avoid reposting shocking photographs without context or permission.
  • Correct stereotypes calmly and explain that refugees come from many countries and backgrounds.

Take action with adults and trusted groups

Ask a teacher about a lesson, book discussion or awareness event. If your school wants to collect money or supplies, work with a registered organisation that explains what communities actually need. Well-intended donations can be wasteful when they are not requested.

Protect dignity and privacy

Never share a person's name, photograph, location or story online without clear permission. Children have a particular right to privacy and protection.

Sources and further learning

UNHCR — Teaching about refugees and UNICEF — Refugee and migrant children.

Try an activity

Use the Safe Passage teamwork game and then discuss why travelling together, finding accurate information and receiving community support matter.
