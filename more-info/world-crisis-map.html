How to understand this refugee crisis map

The red lines are simplified illustrations of displacement connected with Syria, Ukraine, Sudan, Myanmar and Venezuela. They are not live routes, travel directions or a complete record of population movements. Click a route to read a short introduction, then use the sources below for current figures and detailed country information.

Why many people remain near their home countries

International displacement is often regional. Many people first seek safety in a neighbouring country because it is closer, travel may be safer, and family or cultural connections may remain nearby. Others are displaced inside their own country and are known as internally displaced people. A person becomes a refugee only after crossing an international border and meeting the relevant protection definition.

What the map cannot show

Arrows cannot explain individual lives. People may move more than once, return when conditions allow, settle in a host community or require resettlement elsewhere. Conditions and reported totals also change. This map therefore avoids presenting fixed counts and should be used as a starting point for learning rather than as a real-time tracking system.

The people an arrow can miss

A border is not the measure of someone’s loss

Most people forced to flee remain displaced inside their own country. They are called internally displaced people, or IDPs. Because they have not crossed an international border, their movement can be almost invisible on a map focused on refugee routes—even when they have lost a home, school, livelihood and sense of safety.

So when you view each red line, also imagine the journeys that cannot be drawn: a family sheltering in another town, a child changing schools again, or a parent waiting for the day returning home is truly safe. Geography explains where people move; compassion asks us to remember who is moving.

Take the next step: compare this map with the IDMC Global Report on Internal Displacement and the UNHCR Global Trends report, then share the distinction between a refugee and an internally displaced person.

Current data and authoritative sources

Check the UNHCR Refugee Data Finder for reported displacement statistics and the Internal Displacement Monitoring Centre for internal displacement. You can also review our sources and editorial policy and learn the difference between a refugee, migrant, asylum seeker and internally displaced person.
